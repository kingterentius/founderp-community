The field book

Bird, reed and moon

Thoth was drawn as an ibis, wrote on papyrus, and kept the moon. Each plate is cut as a stencil from a real photograph, the way the prints on the clothes are made.

Sacred ibis

Plate I

Threskiornis aethiopicus

The bird of Thoth. It wades the edges of the Nile, where the river meets the desert.

Papyrus

Plate II

Cyperus papyrus

The reed that became paper. Scribes wrote on it, and called Thoth their patron.

The moon of Thout

Plate III

Month I of the old calendar

Thoth kept time by the moon. The first month, Thout, opens each new year and each new drop.

One desert per yearMojave × Los Angeles first. Then Sahara, Namib, Gobi, Kalahari, Atacama.

The name

Deep in thought. Deep in Thoth.

Say it out loud and you hear the state of being lost in your own head. Underneath is Thoth, the Kemetic god of writing, wisdom and the moon, patron of scribes.

The clothes are a place to think: earth-toned, oversized, made for comfort, with one written idea on each piece.
